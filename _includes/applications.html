Applications

How do people use Wallace?

Wallace has been used in high-profile studies to inform biodiversity conservation:

  • Used in global studies for predicting zoonotic disease risk, invasive species, and agricultural pest management, such as khapra beetles, rice stink bugs, and greater banded hornets.
  • A key tool for the BioModelos project in Colombia to build a national atlas of biodiversity and guide monitoring in protected areas.
  • Used by the National Audubon Society to guide priorities for their Flight Plan (Strategic Plan for Conservation Action 2023-2028).
  • Listed as a Global Earth Observations Biodiversity Observation Network BON in a Box Tool and a tool for mapping marine animal forests.
  • Applied in specific species studies including the Curaçaoan Long-nosed Bat, rewilding Colombian giant otters, and forest birds.

The nimble and modular architecture of Wallace has also been used as a template to develop and inspire new open source software:

  • ShinyScholar: A collaboration to create a generalized template for building complex, modular, and reproducible R-based applications (ShinyScholar).
  • Vegetation Analysis: A template for tools analyzing vegetation survey data.
  • National Monitoring: Used by researchers at the Indian Space Research Organization to build a web-based tool for national biodiversity monitoring.

Wallace is also used for teaching and training:

  • Used to teach machine learning and AI concepts to secondary school students.
  • Integrated into the American Museum of Natural History's Science Research Mentoring Program.
